## Design Context

### Users

Kirari is a private companion workspace for one owner, used across desktop, mobile web, and Telegram Mini App. The core jobs are having an ongoing conversation, checking recent shared context, tending long-term memory, and keeping lightweight life events and reminders in one calm place. The interface should feel personal and easy to return to every day, not like an admin dashboard.

### Brand Personality

日系、极简、元气、现代。Kirari should feel quietly attentive, bright, warm, and capable: intimate without being cute or childish, energetic without becoming loud, and technical only where technical detail is genuinely useful.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a crisp cool-gray canvas, deep green-black navigation, pure white work surfaces, one lively vermilion accent, and a restrained acid-yellow highlight. Favor compact Japanese editorial-tool layouts, crisp sans-serif typography, dense ruled rows, sharp alignment, small labels, and asymmetrical color blocks. Keep animation subtle and quick. Avoid beige or paper-like backgrounds, oversized whitespace, oversized headings, large floating cards, decorative hero circles, anime/game UI, candy-color stacking, glassmorphism, purple gradients, generic AI dashboards, and excessive rounded containers.

### Design Principles

1. **Conversation first.** Make chat and the current day feel primary; system maintenance stays available but visually secondary.
2. **Dense structure, bright moments.** Use rules, alignment, and typography for hierarchy; keep spacing compact and reserve vivid color for actions, status, and a few optimistic accents.
3. **Personal, not performative.** Copy and visuals should feel like a private shared room rather than a product marketing surface or enterprise console.
4. **One system at every size.** Preserve every important workflow on small screens with comfortable touch targets, safe areas, and horizontal overflow only where it is intentional.
5. **Clear states.** Loading, empty, focus, disabled, success, and error states must remain legible without relying on color alone.
6. **Chinese typography first.** Use Simplified Chinese glyph forms and locally available Chinese UI fonts before Japanese or Latin fallbacks. Keep Chinese headings at natural tracking, moderate weight, and comfortable line-height; reserve monospaced type for code and short Latin system labels only.
